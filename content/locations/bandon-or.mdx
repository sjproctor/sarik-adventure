summary: |
  In August, we traded the heat and wildfire smoke for a month of cool sea mist in Bandon, a quirky town on the southern Oregon coast.
  Most people aren't familiar with this little blip on the 101, but if you have heard of Bandon, I'd bet you and Erik watch the same golfing YouTube channels. While some of the highest ranking courses in the US sit on the other side of the Coquille River, the town of Bandon is unpretentious and local-centric. It is the embodiment of quiet seaside living where shop hours seem to be based on vibes and everyone has a dog.
overview: |
  Bandon is a fabulously peculiar spot. The locals seem to be either people who work for the massive Bandon Dunes golf complex or retirees enjoying a slower pace of life. It is almost aggressively casual. We repeatedly got comments that our clothing identified us as visitors since locals "don't dress up." This when we were wearing athleisure wear and running shoes.
  The crowning jewel of Bandon is its beautiful beach. It stretches for miles and miles and serves as a canvas for artists, a playground for kids and dogs, a wildlife refuge, and an open space for recreation of all kinds. Each evening, people gather on the overlooks to watch the sunset. On days when it was too windy people sat in their cars but it was clearly a beautiful part of the daily routine and the overlook at the end of our street was always full.

  content: |
    - The whole reason we are here is so Erik can play [Bandon Dunes](https://bandondunesgolf.com/). There are seven golf courses crafted in the Scottish links style. The Scots are golf OGs and consider sandy coastal land the premier base on which to build a course. 
    - [Washed Ashore](https://www.washedashore.org/) is a local non-profit that collects plastic beach trash to create giant sculptures. The pieces are imaginative and impressive but also hold an aura of sadness that these massive constructs are built with what amounts to human carelessness.
    - [Old Town Tap & Grill](https://www.oldtowntapandgrill.com/) is dark and a little weird but has good beer and live music on Saturdays.
    - [Circles in the Sand](https://www.sandypathbandon.com/) is an organization that creates intricate sand labyrinths. People can walk the maze until the tide comes in.

    - **Sea stacks**: The iconic rock formations that define the Oregon Coast are called sea stacks. Over millions of years the wind and waves slowly eroded the softer earth leaving towers of dense rocks formed by harden lava and the immense pressure of the small Juan de Fuca tectonic plate subducting under the massive North American plate. (I'm so obsessed.)
    - **Face Rock**: The famous coastal sea stack shaped like a human profile looking toward the sky. It is said to be Ewauna, the daughter of Chief Siskiyou floating in the ocean with her face tilted up to look at the moon.
