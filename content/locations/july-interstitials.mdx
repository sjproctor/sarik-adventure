summary:
  July was a month of weekend traveling and interstitial stays. We spent a few weeks in Boise visiting our families. Our schedule for the second half of July was dictated by a work twofer. Erik attended Ruby Conf at the Red Rock Casino in Las Vegas and my company hosted a get-together in San Diego the following week. So after short stay in Boise, we packed up Black Betty, the minivan, and headed to south.
  This happens to be Comic Con week in San Diego (IYKYK) so we found a little cabin out in the sticks on Palomar Mountain in northern San Diego county to spend a few days.
overview: |
  In late July, 130,000+ people head to the San Diego convention center to attend Comic Con. The whole downtown area is turned over to art installations, brand activations, vendors, popup events, and sleezy pedicabs. Comic Con used to arrive on our doorstep each year like a cosplay frat party and the only option was to embrace the chaos.
  This year we are lowly outsiders relegated to the edge of San Diego county as the hotels were swept up by visiting superheros and anime characters. So on this in-between-work-events week, we find ourselves up the winding Highway to the Stars on Palomar Mountain way out in the sticks surrounded by pines, birds, and bugs.

  - title: Palomar Observatory
    date: 2026-07-18
    description: |
      The observatory is an active astronomical research center operated by Caltech. It houses the Hale telescope which uses a 200-inch Pyrex parabolic glass plate to focus light from deep space which can be studied by capturing images (originally on glass plates, now digitally) or by analyzing the wavelengths (called spectroscopy). Edwin Hubble took the first image on January 26, 1949 of a nebula named for him.
